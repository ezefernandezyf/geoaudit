Usage statistics

The platform processed a growing number of audits over the last twelve months as more teams adopted the tool for their visibility work. The engineering team shipped the scheduled checks feature in the spring release, which allowed users to monitor their domains continuously instead of running one-off audits.

Onboarding improved significantly after the redesign of the first-run experience. New users now complete the setup flow in a fraction of the time it took before, because the wizard pre-fills the target configuration from the account metadata and the quick start guide covers the common failure modes. The support team also documented the most frequent questions in a public knowledge base that the assistant can cite directly.

The reporting layer generates a client-ready document for every completed audit, including the score breakdown, the prioritized recommendations and the raw evidence for each dimension. Teams use that document both internally and with their stakeholders, so the export format stayed stable across releases even as the underlying scoring model evolved over time.

The pricing model keeps a free tier for individual users, a professional tier for growing teams and an enterprise tier for organizations that need unlimited audits, custom retention and a dedicated success manager. The billing integration handles invoices, seat management and payment method changes without manual intervention, and the customer portal exposes the full subscription history to the account owner.

Adoption grew steadily every quarter, and roughly 20% of the active accounts upgraded to a paid tier during the last renewal cycle.

The roadmap for the coming quarters prioritizes deeper platform integrations, a public API for programmatic audits and multilingual reporting, so the product can serve teams that operate across several markets and publish content in more than one language.

Operational maturity also matters for the larger accounts. The team publishes an uptime dashboard, keeps a public changelog for every release and runs regular security reviews of the infrastructure, so compliance teams can evaluate the platform before they sign the enterprise agreement.

The content team maintains the knowledge base in close coordination with the product team. Every article goes through a review process that checks the technical accuracy, the examples and the screenshots before publication, and outdated pages are retired automatically once the underlying feature changes.
